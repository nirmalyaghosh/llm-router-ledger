# Selat Industries Limited

Annual Report and Accounts for the year ended 31 March 2025

## Chairman's Statement

On behalf of the Board, I am pleased to present the Annual Report and Accounts of Selat Industries Limited for the year ended 31 March 2025.

The Group delivered a resilient performance in FY2025 against a backdrop of subdued industrial demand across several of our end markets. Group revenue increased by 5.5 per cent to S$556.2m, compared with S$527.0m in FY2024 on a restated basis. Operating profit rose to S$82.1m from S$74.6m, an improvement in operating margin from 14.2 per cent to 14.8 per cent. Profit before tax was S$73.7m, up from S$66.7m.

The trading environment during FY2025 was mixed. Demand for naval and commercial marine systems remained robust, underpinned by structural investment in fleet renewal and compliance with tightening emissions standards. Conditions in the industrial and infrastructure markets served by Industrial Coatings were more subdued, with several customers deferring final investment decisions on new projects pending greater economic clarity. Against this backdrop, the Group's ability to grow both revenue and margin reflects the resilience of its long-term contract base and the discipline of its cost management.

Readers of last year's report will recall that Industrial Coatings revenue for FY2024 was originally reported at S$198.7m. Following a review of the timing of revenue recognition on certain long-term coating contracts, this figure has been restated to S$214.6m, as explained more fully in the Financial Review and in Note 4 to the accounts. The restatement affects Industrial Coatings only; Marine Systems revenue for FY2024 is unaffected and remains S$312.4m in both the original and restated presentations.

Both of our reportable segments, Marine Systems and Industrial Coatings, contributed to the year's progress. Marine Systems continued to benefit from sustained demand for propulsion control and navigation systems from naval and commercial shipbuilders, while Industrial Coatings saw steady, if unspectacular, growth as customers in the energy and infrastructure sectors extended the life of existing assets rather than commissioning new ones.

The Board is recommending a final dividend of 9.6 cents per share, giving a total dividend for the year of 14.2 cents per share, up from 13.1 cents in FY2024. This reflects our continued confidence in the Group's cash generation and our commitment to a progressive dividend policy underpinned by dividend cover of at least two times earnings.

Looking further ahead, the Board remains confident in the Group's strategic direction, described in more detail later in this report, and in the underlying demand drivers in both of our segments. We will continue to balance investment for growth with returns to shareholders, while maintaining a conservative approach to the balance sheet.

During the year the Board also spent time considering the Group's longer-term capital allocation priorities, including the balance between organic investment, the dividend and the scope for further debt reduction. Having reviewed these priorities, the Board has concluded that the current progressive dividend policy, together with continued investment in both segments, remains the most appropriate use of the Group's cash generation at this stage of its development.

I would like to thank all Selat employees for their contribution during the year, and shareholders for their continued support.

Tan Boon Huat

## Business Model and Strategy

### Business Model

Selat Industries designs, manufactures and, in the case of Industrial Coatings, applies technically demanding products for customers where reliability and long service life are paramount. The Group's competitive position rests on deep engineering expertise, established relationships with shipbuilders, naval authorities and industrial asset owners, and a track record of performance on long-term contracts. Approximately 60 per cent of Group revenue in FY2025 was derived from contracts with an original duration of more than one year, providing a measure of revenue visibility that the Board regards as a core strength of the business.

The Group's two segments serve distinct but complementary end markets. Marine Systems' customers are principally naval authorities, shipbuilders and commercial vessel operators, largely concentrated in Singapore, the wider Southeast Asian region and, increasingly, the Middle East and Europe. Industrial Coatings' customers are principally owners and operators of energy, pipeline and heavy industrial infrastructure, concentrated in Singapore and the broader Southeast Asian region. Neither segment is materially dependent on a single customer or a single geographic market.

### Strategic Priorities

The Board's strategy rests on three priorities: sustaining organic growth in Marine Systems through continued investment in propulsion control technology; improving the profitability of Industrial Coatings through operational efficiency and selective capacity investment; and maintaining a disciplined capital allocation framework that balances investment, dividends and balance sheet strength. Progress against each of these priorities is reviewed by the Board at least twice a year, and the key performance indicators used to monitor progress include segment revenue growth, segment margin, order book cover and net debt to EBITDA.

Selat Industries traces its origins to a marine instrumentation business founded in the 1950s, with the Industrial Coatings segment added through acquisition in the 1980s. Both segments have since been built up through a combination of organic investment and small, targeted acquisitions of complementary technology and applicator businesses. The Board believes that the combination of the two segments, while serving different end markets, gives the Group a broader base of engineering capability and a more diversified revenue base than either business would have on a standalone basis, while each segment is managed and reported separately.

## Operating Review

### Marine Systems

Marine Systems designs, manufactures and supports propulsion control, navigation and integrated bridge systems for naval, commercial and offshore vessels. The segment operates from three principal sites and employed 2,150 people at 31 March 2025, up from 2,080 a year earlier.

Marine Systems revenue for FY2025 was S$334.9m, up from S$312.4m in FY2024, an increase of 7.2 per cent. This figure is unaffected by the restatement described elsewhere in this report, which relates solely to Industrial Coatings. Segment result for FY2025 was S$61.2m, giving a segment margin of 18.3 per cent, compared with S$54.1m and 17.3 per cent in FY2024.

Growth was driven principally by increased volumes on long-term naval support contracts and by a strong year for retrofit and upgrade work on commercial vessels, as owners sought to comply with tightening emissions standards ahead of the International Maritime Organization's revised timetable. The segment's order book closed the year at S$334.9m, compared with S$298.6m at the end of FY2024, providing strong forward visibility.

During the year the segment secured a five year support contract with a regional naval customer, together with a series of smaller retrofit orders from commercial vessel operators. Marine Systems revenue remains weighted towards Singapore and Southeast Asian naval and commercial customers, though the segment continued to develop its presence in Middle Eastern and European markets during the year, principally through service and support arrangements rather than new-build contracts. The segment's lost-time injury frequency rate improved for the third consecutive year, reflecting continued investment in workplace safety.

Capital expenditure in the segment was S$22.1m, principally on machine tools and test rig capacity at the Group's Tuas facility, compared with S$19.8m in FY2024. Segment assets at the year end were S$412.6m, compared with S$389.4m at 31 March 2024.

Research and development spend within Marine Systems was S$11.8m in FY2025, compared with S$10.4m in FY2024, focused principally on the next generation of propulsion control software and on sensor integration for autonomous and remotely operated vessel systems. Management expects this area of investment to grow moderately as a proportion of segment revenue over the medium term, reflecting customer demand for increasingly sophisticated integrated systems rather than standalone components.

We continue to see Marine Systems as the Group's primary growth engine over the medium term, supported by structural demand for fleet modernisation among both naval and commercial customers.

### Industrial Coatings

Industrial Coatings manufactures and applies protective and functional coatings for infrastructure, energy and heavy industrial customers, including long-term maintenance and protection contracts for pipelines, storage tanks and offshore structures.

Industrial Coatings revenue for FY2025 was S$221.3m, compared with a restated S$214.6m in FY2024, an increase of 3.1 per cent. As originally reported at the time of last year's Annual Report, FY2024 Industrial Coatings revenue was S$198.7m; the reasons for the subsequent restatement to S$214.6m are explained in the Financial Review and in Note 4. On a like-for-like basis using the restated comparative, growth was modest, reflecting continued caution among energy sector customers regarding new capital projects.

Segment result for FY2025 was S$32.4m, a margin of 14.6 per cent, compared with a restated S$31.0m in FY2024. The segment employed 1,480 people at the year end, up from 1,410, and its order book closed the year at S$186.4m, compared with S$171.9m at 31 March 2024.

The segment was awarded a three year maintenance framework contract with a major pipeline operator during the year, together with a number of smaller tank lining and refurbishment contracts. Management continued to focus on improving the mix of longer-duration contracts within the segment, consistent with the Group's broader strategic priorities. Product quality and application consistency remain key differentiators for the segment, and the investment at Jurong Island described below is intended to support further improvement in both areas over the next two years.

Capital expenditure in the segment was S$14.6m, including the first phase of the upgrade to the Group's Jurong Island application facility, compared with S$12.3m in FY2024. Segment assets at the year end were S$268.9m, compared with a restated S$251.2m at 31 March 2024.

The segment continues to see good demand for maintenance and life-extension work on existing infrastructure, and management expects the completed facility upgrade to support margin improvement in future periods.

Customer concentration within Industrial Coatings remains higher than within Marine Systems, with the segment's ten largest customers together accounting for approximately 45 per cent of segment revenue in FY2025, broadly consistent with FY2024. Management continues to pursue a broader customer base as part of its efforts to reduce this concentration over time, while maintaining the depth of relationship with key accounts that underpins the segment's long-term maintenance contract base.

Product development within Industrial Coatings during the year focused on lower-emission formulations and on coatings with extended recoating intervals, both of which are increasingly specified by customers seeking to reduce lifecycle costs and environmental impact. The segment's technical services team also expanded its remote inspection capability, using drone and sensor-based survey techniques to support customers' asset integrity programmes, a service that management expects to become a more significant contributor to segment revenue over time.

This compares with restated Group revenue of S$527.0m for FY2024, comprising S$312.4m from Marine Systems and S$214.6m from Industrial Coatings. As originally reported for FY2024, Group revenue was S$511.1m, comprising the same S$312.4m from Marine Systems and S$198.7m from Industrial Coatings before restatement.

Gross profit for FY2025 was S$214.8m, a margin of 38.6 per cent, compared with S$202.1m and 38.4 per cent on a restated basis in FY2024. Administrative and distribution expenses were S$132.7m, compared with S$127.5m, giving operating profit of S$82.1m for FY2025 compared with a restated S$74.6m for FY2024. There were no exceptional items in either FY2025 or FY2024.

Net finance costs were S$8.4m, comprising finance costs of S$9.8m less finance income of S$1.4m, compared with S$7.9m in FY2024, comprising finance costs of S$9.1m less finance income of S$1.2m. Basic earnings per share were 38.4 cents for FY2025, based on a weighted average of 150.6 million shares in issue, compared with a restated 34.9 cents for FY2024 and an originally reported 29.5 cents for FY2024, both based on a weighted average of 150.4 million shares.

The mix of Group revenue between the two segments shifted slightly during the year, with Marine Systems representing 60.2 per cent of Group revenue in FY2025 compared with 59.3 per cent on a restated basis in FY2024, and Industrial Coatings representing the balance of 39.8 per cent and 40.7 per cent respectively. ## Notes to the Accounts

The note below forms an integral part of the financial statements and should be read together with the Financial Review and Segmental Analysis sections of this report.

### Note 4: Restatement of Comparative Information

During FY2025, management reassessed a population of long-term coating contracts within Industrial Coatings and concluded that certain contracts previously accounted for on a point-in-time basis, with revenue recognised on customer acceptance, in fact met the criteria for over-time recognition, because the customer controls the asset as coating work progresses and the Group has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date. Revenue on these contracts has accordingly been restated to be recognised over time using a cost-based input method, consistent with the Group's revenue recognition policy.

The effect of this restatement on the FY2024 comparative was to increase Industrial Coatings revenue from S$198.7m as originally reported to a restated S$214.6m, an increase of S$15.9m, and to increase Industrial Coatings segment result from S$20.8m as originally reported to a restated S$31.0m, an increase of S$10.2m. Group revenue for FY2024 increased from S$511.1m to S$527.0m, and Group profit before tax increased from S$56.5m to S$66.7m. Marine Systems figures for FY2024 are unaffected. The restatement is a matter of timing only, does not affect total contract revenue recognised over the life of the affected contracts, and had no effect on cash flows, net debt or the Group's banking covenants in either year.

The Audit Committee reviewed management's assessment of the affected contract population, including the criteria applied in determining whether each contract met the requirements for revenue recognition over time, and concluded that the restatement had been appropriately identified, quantified and disclosed. No comparative periods prior to FY2024 have been restated, as the affected contracts were not material to Industrial Coatings revenue in earlier years.
